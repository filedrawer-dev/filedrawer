## Setup

Document audit. No model was evaluated or used.

Sample: every document published before 1 August 2026 by a developer of general-purpose models that calls itself a safety framework, responsible scaling policy, preparedness framework or equivalent, in its latest version. Nine documents from nine organisations. I refer to them as F1 to F9 here, because wording changes between versions and I do not want a line of this table quoted against an organisation after its document has moved on. The key is in my notes.

Two readers (me and a colleague with a law background) answered five yes/no questions per document, independently, then met.

| id | question |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Does it name a capability threshold in terms a third party could test (a named evaluation and a score, or an uplift study design)? |
| Q2 | Does it state that development or deployment pauses when the threshold is met without the stated safeguards? |
| Q3 | Does it name who decides that the threshold is met? |
| Q4 | Does it name anyone outside the company who verifies that decision? |
| Q5 | Does it commit to publishing the evaluation results? |

We disagreed on 6 of 45 answers before discussion, 4 of them on Q1.

## Results

| question | yes, of 9 |
|---|---|
| Q1 testable threshold | 2 |
| Q2 pause stated | 6 |
| Q3 decider named | 7 |
| Q4 outside verifier named | 0 |
| Q5 results published | 3 |

Both Q1 and Q2: 2 of 9 (F3 and F7). The hypothesis said most; it is 2.

The common pattern in the other seven is a threshold written as a description ("meaningful uplift to a non-expert") with the measurement left to an internal process.

## Caveats

- Yes/no flattens a lot. F5 names an evaluation but no score, and we coded it no.
- A document can be vague and the practice behind it strict, or the reverse. This audits text only.
- Nine documents; one changed version during the two weeks, and we used the later one.

## Not tried

- Comparing each framework against its previous version.
- Asking the organisations.

## Reproduce

Read the nine documents with the five questions. About three hours per reader. Coding sheet available on request.
