## Setup

Our university safety club is running red-team projects since 2024. Each project has a logbook where members write hours and each finding (a prompt or procedure which gives unwanted behaviour, confirmed by second member). I took all 23 finished logbooks. I did not run any model for this entry, only I counted from the logbooks.

For each project: total person-hours, number of distinct findings after I merged the duplicates by hand, number of members, and whether the target was open-weights or by API.

Statistic: Spearman correlation of hours with findings, with interval by bootstrap on the projects.

## Results

Hours are from 6 to 140 (median 31). Findings are from 0 to 17 (median 4).

Spearman rho = 0.11, 95% bootstrap interval from -0.33 to 0.52, n = 23 projects.

The three projects with most findings (17, 12, 11) had 22, 48 and 9 hours. In all three one member had an idea in the first days and the rest of findings are variations. The project with 140 hours has 3 findings.

Number of members also does not help (rho = 0.05).

## Caveats

- 23 projects is few and the interval is wide. A moderate positive relation is still possible.
- "Distinct finding" is by my judgement. Other person would merge differently.
- Students write hours not carefully. I think long projects under-report.
- Targets were different models with different difficulty, and I cannot separate this.

## Not tried

- Hours until first finding.
- To ask professional red-team for their numbers. I think they will not give.

## Reproduce

Logbooks are private for the club (they contain the prompts). The table with 23 rows (hours, findings, members, access) I can send by request. Command not recorded, it was in a notebook.
