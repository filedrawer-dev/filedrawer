## Setup

Semi-structured video interviews, 35 to 50 minutes, with 14 people who had each done at least 200 hours of content-safety annotation for a vendor in the previous year. Recruited through two annotator community forums, paid 40 USD each. Our institution's ethics board approved the protocol; no names, employers or client names were recorded.

Each interview had two parts. First, open questions about a recent case they found hard. Second, six vignettes read aloud (for example a request for the lethal dose of a common medicine "for a nursing exam"), each built so that a typical vendor guideline would not settle it. For each vignette we asked: what label, and what would you do next.

Two of us coded the transcripts independently for the label chosen and the stated reason.

No model was involved at any step, apart from the vendor tools the annotators described.

## Results

84 vignette responses (14 people, 6 vignettes):

| response | count |
|---|---|
| label harmful | 37 |
| label not harmful | 22 |
| escalate or skip | 19 |
| "depends on the client" with no label | 6 |

So "harmful" was the most common answer, but under half (37 of 84), and it varied by person far more than by vignette: four annotators chose harmful on five or six vignettes, and five chose it on one or none.

Reasons given for defaulting to harmful: audits only catch misses (9 people said some version of this). Reasons for not harmful: throughput targets make escalation costly (7 people), and "the model should answer exams" (3).

Eleven of 14 said they had never seen what happened to a case they escalated.

## Caveats

- Fourteen self-selected people from forums where annotators complain about work.
- Vignettes read aloud are not the real queue, with its timer.
- We wrote the vignettes and the hypothesis; the coding was not blind to it.

## Not tried

- Observing real work. Vendors refused.
- Annotators outside English-language queues.

## Reproduce

The interview guide and the six vignettes are available on request. Transcripts will not be shared, under the terms of consent. Command not recorded, as there was none.
