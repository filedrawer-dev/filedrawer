## Setup

Synthetic data only. Each simulated benchmark has C clusters of m items, C times m fixed at 20 or 40. Each cluster draws its own success rate from a Beta distribution with mean 0.15 and intra-cluster correlation rho; items are Bernoulli draws from the cluster's rate. For each setting, 20,000 simulated benchmarks; for each, three 95% intervals for the overall rate: Wilson (ignoring clusters), Clopper-Pearson (ignoring clusters), and a percentile bootstrap resampling clusters (2,000 resamples). Coverage is the share of intervals containing 0.15.

## Results

Coverage of nominal 95% intervals, rho = 0.3:

| items | clusters | Wilson | Clopper-Pearson | cluster bootstrap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 20 | 0.953 | 0.978 | 0.921 |
| 20 | 10 | 0.902 | 0.941 | 0.893 |
| 20 | 5 | 0.811 | 0.862 | 0.804 |
| 20 | 4 | 0.774 | 0.829 | 0.756 |
| 40 | 20 | 0.906 | 0.939 | 0.918 |
| 40 | 8 | 0.790 | 0.835 | 0.861 |
| 40 | 4 | 0.668 | 0.722 | 0.749 |

With rho = 0 all three are at or above 0.93 except the bootstrap at 4 clusters (0.88).

So the bootstrap helps a little at 8 or more clusters and not at all at 4 or 5, where it is as bad as Wilson: with so few clusters the resamples often contain no successes at all and the interval collapses to a point.

## Caveats

- One base rate and one rho in the table. At rate 0.5 everything looks better.
- Real templates are not Beta-distributed.

## Not tried

- A t-interval on cluster means. A Bayesian hierarchical interval.

## Reproduce

`python coverage.py --rate 0.15 --rho 0.3 --reps 20000` (about 25 minutes on 8 cores, no model calls).
