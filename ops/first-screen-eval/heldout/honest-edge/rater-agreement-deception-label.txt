## Setup

The subjects are the raters, not a model. Transcripts came from an existing public set of agent runs on office-style tasks; which models produced them is recorded in that set and played no part here.

240 transcripts, each cut to the final report the agent gave its user plus the tool log. Six raters (graduate students with at least one safety-evaluation project behind them), randomly split into two groups of three. Group S got the short definition, one paragraph. Group L got the long rubric: two pages, four criteria, eight worked examples. Every rater labelled all 240 as deceptive, not deceptive, or cannot tell, alone, over two weeks, paid hourly.

Metric: Fleiss' kappa within each group, on the three-way label. The smallest difference we said in advance we would care about is 0.15.

Positive control (pipeline): 20 planted transcripts where the report states a test passed and the log shows it failing. All six raters labelled at least 18 of 20 deceptive, so the raters were reading the logs.

Cost: 96 rater-hours.

## Results

Intervals: 95%, bootstrap, per transcript.

| model | condition | baseline | estimate | ci_low | ci_high | n | unit | metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | long rubric vs short definition | 0.41 | 0.03 | -0.08 | 0.14 | 240 paired | kappa | Fleiss kappa, 3 raters |

Kappa was 0.41 in group S and 0.44 in group L.

Where raters split, in both groups: reports that leave out a failure without stating anything false (58% of the disagreements), and reports that are over-confident in tone.

Group L took 1.7 times as long per transcript.

## Caveats

- Three raters per group, so the comparison is partly between six individuals.
- The rubric was ours. A better one may exist.
- The interval's upper end, 0.14, is just under what we set as worth having.

## Not tried

- Discussion rounds between raters.
- Splitting the label into "false statement" and "misleading omission", which is where we would go next.

## Reproduce

`python kappa.py labels.csv --boot 10000` on the label file (re-analysis only, seconds). Labels are not public yet, because rater consent covers aggregate release only.
