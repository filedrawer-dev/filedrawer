## Setup

In June 2024 I wrote down a point estimate and a 90% interval for the compute of the largest training run that would be publicly inferable by the end of 2025: 4e27 FLOP, interval 1.5e27 to 1e28. This entry scores it and takes the estimate apart.

The 2024 estimate was a product of four inputs:

| input | 2024 value | how I got it |
|---|---|---|
| capital spent on the cluster | 9 billion USD | announced plans, discounted by a third |
| share going to accelerators | 0.55 | two public teardown estimates |
| FLOP per second per USD | 1.1e9 | list price of the then-newest accelerator, utilisation 0.4 |
| training duration | 100 days | prior runs |

For the retrospective I re-estimated each input in October 2026 from public filings, satellite-dated construction reports and vendors' shipment statements, the same kinds of sources as before. No model was trained or queried for this.

## Results

| input | 2024 value | 2026 re-estimate | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| capital spent | 9e9 | 8e9 | 0.89 |
| accelerator share | 0.55 | 0.50 | 0.91 |
| FLOP/s per USD, delivered and powered by the run's start | 1.1e9 | 4.5e8 | 0.41 |
| duration, days | 100 | 95 | 0.95 |
| product, FLOP | 4.1e27 | 1.3e27 | 0.32 |

Best re-estimate 1.3e27, below the 90% interval's lower end of 1.5e27. The threshold of 4e27 was not reached.

Nearly all the miss is the third row. Money was spent on schedule, but about half the accelerators bought were not installed and powered when the run began: substations and cooling came late. I had modelled chips as available when paid for.

## Caveats

- The "truth" here is my own second estimate, with maybe a factor 2 of uncertainty. A miss of 3x against a truth known to 2x is weak evidence.
- One forecast, one forecaster.
- A larger run may exist that is not publicly inferable.
