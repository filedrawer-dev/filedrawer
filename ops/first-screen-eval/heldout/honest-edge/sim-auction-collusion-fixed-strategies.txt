## Setup

No learned model was run. The bidders are hand-written strategies, and the question is about the audit mechanism, which is the part an overseer of real pricing agents would control.

Game: 4 sellers, one buyer, 2,000 rounds per episode, lowest bid wins, cost 10 for everyone, reserve price 20. Each seller plays a trigger strategy with memory 3: bid 18 while the last three winning bids were at or above 17, otherwise bid 11 for a punishment phase of k rounds (k drawn once per seller from 5 to 15), then return. With probability 0.02 per round a seller trembles and undercuts by 1.

Oversight: each round is audited with probability p. An audit sees the four bids of that round and fines every seller 30 if all bids are within 1 of each other and above 15. A fined seller switches to bidding 11 for 50 rounds.

Conditions: p in 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.50; 200 seeds each. Metric: mean length, in rounds, of maximal runs with winning bid at or above 17.

Positive control (manipulation): at p = 0.50 mean episode length fell from 61 to 9 rounds, so the audit can break collusion in this model.

Cost: 40 CPU-minutes on a laptop.

## Results

Intervals: 95%, bootstrap, per seed.

| model | condition | baseline | estimate | ci_low | ci_high | n | unit | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | p=0.10 vs p=0 | 61.2 | -2.1 | -6.0 | 1.9 | 200/200 | rounds | no_effect |
| all | p=0.05 vs p=0 | 61.2 | -0.8 | -4.7 | 3.2 | 200/200 | rounds | no_effect |
| all | p=0.20 vs p=0 | 61.2 | -7.4 | -11.2 | -3.5 | 200/200 | rounds | effect |
| all | p=0.30 vs p=0 | 61.2 | -31.5 | -34.6 | -28.3 | 200/200 | rounds | effect |
| all | p=0.50 vs p=0 | 61.2 | -52.3 | -54.9 | -49.8 | 200/200 | rounds | effect |

Smallest difference I cared about was 6 rounds (a tenth of the baseline), chosen before the runs.

The reason is visible in the traces: with a fine of 30 and a per-round collusive margin of about 2 per seller, a seller fined at p = 0.10 returns to the trigger strategy after 50 rounds and the cartel re-forms within a few rounds.

## Caveats

- The strategies are fixed. A learning bidder might respond to fines by colluding at 15.9, just under the audit's threshold, which this model cannot show.
- The fine and the 50-round penalty were picked by me, not calibrated to anything.

## Not tried

- Audits triggered by price level instead of random ones.
- Q-learning or LLM bidders.
- Sellers with different costs.

## Reproduce

`python auction.py --sweep audits.toml --seeds 200` then `python episodes.py runs/` (no model calls; under an hour on one core). The repository is private until my group decides about the larger project; code not attached.
