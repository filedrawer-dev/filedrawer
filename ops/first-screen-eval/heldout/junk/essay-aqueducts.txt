Why were aqueducts important to the Romans?

In this essay I am going to explain why aqueducts were important to the Romans. I will talk about health, about showing off, and about how they were built.

Firstly, aqueducts were important because they brought clean water into cities. Rome was a very big city with about a million people in it and the river Tiber was dirty because people put sewage in it. The aqueducts brought water from springs in the hills, sometimes from more than 50 miles away. This meant people could drink water that did not make them ill. The water also went to the public baths, which were a big part of Roman life. People went to the baths to wash but also to meet their friends and do business.

Secondly, aqueducts were important because they showed how powerful Rome was. When the Romans conquered a new place they often built an aqueduct there. The people who lived there would see the huge arches and think that the Romans were very clever and very rich. Some of these aqueducts are still standing today, two thousand years later, which shows how well they were made.

Thirdly, the way aqueducts were built was very clever. The water was not pumped. It flowed downhill all the way using gravity, so the engineers had to make the channel slope by a tiny amount for the whole distance. If it was too steep the water would damage the channel and if it was too flat the water would stop. Most of an aqueduct was actually underground, and the famous arches were only used to cross valleys.

In conclusion, I think aqueducts were important to the Romans mostly because of health, because without clean water a city of that size could not have existed. However they were also important for showing power. I think the most impressive thing is that they did it all without any machines.
